The details, in plain English

Short answers to the questions people ask most before they pay.

One of the five pillars of Islam. If your qualifying wealth has stayed above the nisab for a full lunar year (the hawl), you give 2.5% of it to those eligible to receive it — the eight categories named in Qur'an 9:60. It is not charity you choose to give; it is a right owed to those in need.

The minimum threshold above which Zakat becomes due: 612.36 grams of silver (£896.93) or 87.48 grams of gold (£8,885.68) as of 09/10/2026. Most UK institutions use the silver standard, because it lowers the threshold so more people pay and more households are helped. This calculator defaults to silver for exactly that reason; switch to gold in step 05 if you follow that view.

Debts that fall due within your zakat year: credit cards, overdrafts, unpaid bills, rent arrears and personal loans. For a long mortgage, many scholars allow deducting roughly the next twelve months of payments rather than the whole balance. Student loans are treated differently by different scholars — ask someone knowledgeable if yours is significant.

Opinions differ. Many scholars count pensions you can access or transfer (an old workplace or personal pension, for example), sometimes reduced for tax. Others exempt pensions entirely until withdrawal. If a pension is a large part of your wealth, ask a scholar you trust — and simply leave the field at zero if you follow the exempt view.

Pick a date on the lunar calendar when your wealth first exceeded the nisab — many people choose Ramadan — and recalculate on that anniversary each year, paying promptly. You may pay earlier than due, especially to reach people in urgent need.

Jakat offers general guidance, not a fatwa or financial advice. Figures depend on the rates you enter — check gold and silver prices on the day you pay, and consult a knowledgeable scholar about your own circumstances.
